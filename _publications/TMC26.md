excerpt: 'Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) are driving the emergence of multi-modal and agentic services for mobile users through cloud and edge infrastructures, where long-context workloads pose daunting challenges for inference latency. Existing disaggregated LLM serving systems largely rely on hardware profiling, configuration enumeration, or heuristic scheduling, offering limited analytical guidance for cost-efficient resource allocation. In this paper, we propose SARA, a Service-level objective (SLO)-Aware Resource Allocation framework for disaggregated LLM inference consisting of cloud prefill, key-value (KV) cache transfer, and edge decode stages. It maximizes goodput under a deployment cost budget and a series of quantile-based SLO constraints. By capitalizing on queuing theory and a generalized birth-death process, we characterize the cloud prefill, KV cache transfer, and edge decode stages of the disaggregated inference. These characterizations explicitly map system parameters to minimum resource requirements, and reveal that stringent SLOs and heavy-tailed workloads substantially amplify resource demands beyond the stability conditions. Finally, we develop an effective resource allocation framework to maximize system goodput under a joint budget for cloud prefill, cache transfer bandwidth, and edge decode resources. Simulation and hardware results demonstrate mean errors below 5%, and an average goodput improvement of 26.6% over state-of-the-art baselines under the same deployment cost.'
